The height of a node is the number of consecutive heads before the first tails in repeated fair coin flips.

Why are skiplists fast?

Every node appears at the bottom level L0. A node of height k also appears in L1 through Lk, forming a tower. Higher levels let a search skip many bottom-level nodes. The sentinel is a dummy start node, not a stored value.

Section 4.4 · Analysis

Expected, not guaranteed

For fair coin flips, P(height ≥ r) = 2−r. Morin proves O(log n) expected path length and O(n) expected space; exceptionally tall towers or long searches remain possible.

The displayed heights and examples are illustrative realizations, not a guarantee that every skiplist has logarithmic search paths.
