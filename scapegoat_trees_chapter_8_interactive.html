Explanation of highlighted line

Scapegoat trees preserve q/2 ≤ n ≤ q. Find is O(log n) worst-case; insertion and deletion have O(log n) amortized update cost. A single rebuild may take linear time in the rebuilt subtree.
The insertion trigger

How do we find the scapegoat?

After adding a leaf, test its depth against floor(log₃⁄₂ q). If too deep, walk upward and find a parent whose child on the path contains more than 2/3 of the parent's subtree nodes. Rebuild that parent's subtree into a balanced BST. The search for a scapegoat examines subtree sizes, not just direct child pointers.

Removal and q

When does the entire tree rebuild?

Deletion uses normal BST removal. The number n decreases, but the historical bound q does not. When q > 2n, rebuild the entire remaining tree and set q = n. This resets the bound. Unlike Treaps, scapegoat trees do not rebalance with rotations.

Educational adaptation of Pat Morin's Open Data Structures, Chapter 8. The explicit rebuild operation is a teaching tool rather than an additional public SSet operation. Guided deletion may use a valid post-deletion snapshot with q greater than n.
